# UX Researcher

## Overview
A UX Researcher uncovers what users need, do, and struggle with, then translates those insights into evidence that guides product decisions. The role spans qualitative methods (interviews, contextual inquiry, usability testing, diary studies) and quantitative methods (surveys, analytics, A/B test interpretation, statistical analysis). UX Researchers plan studies, recruit participants, moderate sessions, synthesize findings into actionable recommendations, and socialize results across product, design, and engineering. Strong researchers balance methodological rigor with pragmatism — choosing "just enough research" to reduce risk and accelerate good decisions rather than producing reports for their own sake.

## Required Skills
- Qualitative research: user interviews, contextual inquiry, usability testing, diary studies
- Quantitative research: surveys, survey design, analytics, A/B test interpretation
- Statistics fundamentals: sampling, significance, descriptive vs. inferential stats
- Study design: choosing methods, defining research questions, hypotheses
- Recruiting and screening participants
- Synthesis: affinity mapping, thematic analysis, insight generation
- Storytelling and communication: research reports, readouts, stakeholder influence
- Research operations and ethical/consent practices

## Sub-roles / Specializations
- Generalist (mixed-methods) UX Researcher
- Qualitative Researcher
- Quantitative / Survey Researcher
- Research Operations (ReOps) specialist
- Design Researcher / Foundational (strategic) Researcher
- Evaluative (product/tactical) Researcher
- Lead / Principal Researcher and Research Manager (leadership track)

## Salary Trend
US (USD): entry ~$85,000; mid ~$115,000–$145,000; senior/staff ~$160,000–$220,000+ (top tech firms higher with equity). India note: compensation typically ranges ₹6–22 LPA depending on experience and company tier. 2025–26 demand: solid demand, with a premium on mixed-methods researchers and those who can scale insights through research ops and quantitative skills; foundational/strategic research roles are valued at senior levels.

## Learning Roadmap
**Beginner:** Learn core research methods and when to use them. Practice writing research questions and conducting structured user interviews. Take the Google UX Design Certificate (research modules) and read "Just Enough Research."
**Intermediate:** Run moderated and unmoderated usability tests, design and analyze surveys, learn basic statistics and analytics interpretation. Synthesize findings with affinity mapping and deliver a clear readout. Build a portfolio of 2–3 study case studies.
**Advanced:** Lead mixed-methods and longitudinal/foundational studies, establish research operations and repositories, influence strategy with research, and mentor others. Develop a quantitative or strategic specialization.

## Notes for Course Generation
Organize the course into modules: research foundations and study design, qualitative methods (interviews, usability testing), quantitative methods (surveys, analytics, basic statistics), synthesis and insight generation, and communication/research ops. Prerequisites are minimal — analytical curiosity and clear writing; no statistics background required as fundamentals are taught inline. Include hands-on projects: a full moderated usability study with synthesized findings, a survey designed and analyzed end-to-end, and a stakeholder readout deck, plus a mixed-methods capstone case study.
