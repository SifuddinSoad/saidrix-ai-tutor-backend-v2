# Engineering Manager

## Overview
An Engineering Manager (EM) leads a team of software engineers, accountable for both the people and the delivery. The role blends people leadership (hiring, growth, performance, retention, regular 1:1s), delivery management (planning, execution, quality, removing blockers), and technical strategy (architecture direction, technical debt, prioritization in partnership with product). EMs typically transition from senior engineering roles and gradually shift from writing code to multiplying the team's impact through coaching, organizational design, and decision-making. Success is measured by team health, sustained delivery, and the growth of the people on the team.

## Required Skills
- People leadership: coaching, mentoring, career development, effective 1:1s
- Performance management: feedback, reviews, handling underperformance
- Hiring: interviewing, building inclusive teams, onboarding
- Delivery management: planning, execution, unblocking, stakeholder alignment
- Technical strategy: architecture awareness, technical debt and prioritization
- Communication: upward, cross-functional, and team communication
- Organizational and team design; conflict resolution
- Emotional intelligence and trust-building

## Sub-roles / Specializations
- Frontline Engineering Manager (single team)
- Manager of Managers / Senior EM
- Director of Engineering
- VP of Engineering (leadership track)
- Specialized EM (platform, infrastructure, ML, security teams)
- Engineering leadership with adjacent EM/Staff-IC dual-track context

## Salary Trend
US (USD): entry/new EM ~$140,000; mid ~$180,000–$230,000; senior/director ~$260,000–$400,000+ (large tech firms substantially higher with equity). India note: compensation typically ranges ₹25–70 LPA depending on level, company tier, and experience. 2025–26 demand: stable; demand favors EMs who can balance delivery with people growth, lead through ambiguity, and manage hybrid/distributed teams, with platform and ML leadership at a premium.

## Learning Roadmap
**Beginner:** Understand the manager role and the IC-to-manager transition. Learn to run effective 1:1s, give feedback, and delegate. Read "The Manager's Path" (early chapters) and shadow an experienced manager.
**Intermediate:** Manage a small team end-to-end: hiring, performance conversations, delivery planning, and stakeholder communication. Learn team health metrics and handle conflict. Study "An Elegant Puzzle" and engage with LeadDev content.
**Advanced:** Lead larger or multiple teams, design org structures, set technical and people strategy, manage managers, and drive change through ambiguity. Develop executive communication and long-range planning skills.

## Notes for Course Generation
Structure the course in modules: the IC-to-manager transition, people leadership (1:1s, feedback, growth), hiring and team building, delivery and execution management, and technical/organizational strategy. Prerequisites: senior-level engineering experience or equivalent technical credibility is strongly recommended. Include hands-on projects/case studies: role-play 1:1 and feedback scenarios, a hiring plan and structured interview rubric, a team health and delivery assessment, and a capstone case study turning around a struggling team or scaling a growing one.
