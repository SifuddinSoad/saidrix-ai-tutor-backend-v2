# Data Scientist

## Overview
A Data Scientist applies statistics, machine learning, and experimentation to extract predictive and causal insight from data. Day-to-day work spans framing problems, building and validating models, designing experiments (A/B tests), and communicating quantified recommendations to product and business teams. The role blends rigorous analysis with software skills and domain understanding, and increasingly expects fluency with generative-AI tooling alongside classical modeling.

## Required Skills
- **Python** — pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn (or strong R)
- **Statistics & probability** — inference, regression, Bayesian basics, sampling
- **Machine learning** — supervised/unsupervised methods, model evaluation, cross-validation
- **Experimentation & causal inference** — A/B testing, confounding, causal reasoning
- **SQL** — data extraction and wrangling at scale
- **Feature engineering & model interpretation** — building, debugging, and explaining models
- **Data visualization & communication** — turning model output into decisions
- **Reproducible workflow** — notebooks, Git, environments, experiment hygiene

## Sub-roles / Specializations
- Product Data Scientist (experimentation / metrics focus)
- ML / Modeling Data Scientist
- Causal Inference / Econometrics Scientist
- Research Scientist (deep learning, NLP, CV)
- Decision Scientist / Analytics Scientist
- Domain-specific (bioinformatics, fraud, recommendation)

## Salary Trend
US (USD): entry ~$110k; mid ~$140k–$160k; senior ~$190k–$250k+ (higher at FAANG-tier firms). India: typically ₹8–25 LPA depending on company and specialization. 2025–26 demand: solid but more selective than the 2018–2021 boom; employers favor candidates who ship production-relevant models, reason about experimentation, and have generative-AI fluency.

## Learning Roadmap
**Beginner:** Python programming; statistics and probability foundations; pandas/NumPy; basic visualization; the ML mindset (train/validation/test).
**Intermediate:** scikit-learn end-to-end (regression, classification, clustering); proper model evaluation; feature engineering; SQL at scale; A/B testing fundamentals.
**Advanced:** Causal inference; ensemble methods and gradient boosting; intro deep learning; experimentation design at scale; MLOps awareness; translating models into business decisions.

## Notes for Course Generation
Anchor the course in a problem-first arc: each module starts with a business or research question, introduces the statistical or ML technique needed, and ends with model evaluation plus a written recommendation. Prerequisites are Python and basic statistics; data-analyst-level SQL helps. Stress experimentation and causal thinking (not just predictive accuracy), and include projects on tabular prediction, an A/B test analysis, and a model with an interpretation writeup for a portfolio.
