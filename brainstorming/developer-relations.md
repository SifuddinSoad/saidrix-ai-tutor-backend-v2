# Developer Relations (DevRel) / Developer Advocate

## Overview
A Developer Relations professional (Developer Advocate / DevRel Engineer) builds and sustains the relationship between a company and the developers who use its products and APIs. The role blends technical depth with communication: producing tutorials and sample code, speaking at conferences, growing and supporting communities, and carrying developer feedback back into the product roadmap. DevRel sits at the intersection of engineering, marketing, product, and community, and success is measured through developer adoption, satisfaction, and the strength of the feedback loop rather than direct sales.

## Required Skills
- Solid hands-on engineering ability to build credible demos, sample apps, and SDK examples
- Technical content creation: tutorials, blog posts, documentation, and video
- Public speaking, presentations, and live coding/demos
- Community building and management (forums, Discord/Slack, events, open source)
- Developer empathy and the ability to translate feedback into actionable product input
- Strong written and verbal communication for technical audiences
- Familiarity with APIs, SDKs, CLIs, and the developer tooling ecosystem
- Metrics and storytelling: measuring and communicating DevRel impact
- Cross-functional collaboration with product, engineering, and marketing
- Social media and online community engagement

## Sub-roles / Specializations
- Developer Advocate / Evangelist (content and speaking focus)
- Developer Experience (DX) Engineer (SDKs, docs, onboarding flows)
- Community Manager (forums, events, programs)
- Developer Content / Education Lead
- Developer Marketing Specialist
- DevRel Program Manager / Operations
- Head of DevRel / Developer Ecosystem

## Salary Trend
US (USD): entry ~$90,000; mid ~$130,000-$160,000; senior ~$180,000-$260,000+ (leadership and staff-level advocate roles trend toward the higher end). India: roughly ₹10-30 LPA depending on seniority and global scope. 2025-26 demand: solid for API-first, developer-tooling, AI/ML, and cloud companies, where developer adoption drives revenue; roles increasingly expect measurable impact and genuine engineering credibility.

## Learning Roadmap
**Beginner:** Build solid engineering fundamentals and a public portfolio (projects, blog posts); learn technical writing basics and start sharing small tutorials or talks.
**Intermediate:** Create end-to-end tutorials and sample apps for a real API/SDK, give meetup or conference talks, and actively participate in a developer community.
**Advanced:** Run developer programs and events, define DevRel strategy and metrics, build feedback loops into product, and lead community and content initiatives at scale.

## Notes for Course Generation
Structure a course as: engineering credibility and portfolio building, technical content creation, public speaking and live demos, community building, and DevRel metrics and product feedback loops. Prerequisites are working programming ability and basic familiarity with APIs and version control. Capstone projects should include a polished end-to-end API tutorial with sample code, a recorded technical talk or demo, and a written DevRel strategy with success metrics.
