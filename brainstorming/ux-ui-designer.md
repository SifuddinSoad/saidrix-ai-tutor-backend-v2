# UX/UI Designer

## Overview
A UX/UI Designer shapes how people experience and interact with digital products. The UX (user experience) side focuses on understanding users, defining information architecture, mapping flows, and validating designs through research and usability testing. The UI (user interface) side focuses on visual design, interaction design, typography, color, layout, and building consistent, accessible design systems. In many organizations the two are combined into a single product design role that takes a problem from research and wireframes through high-fidelity prototypes to a polished, accessible, shipped interface in close partnership with product managers and engineers.

## Required Skills
- UX research literacy: user interviews, usability testing, persona and journey mapping
- Information architecture: site maps, navigation, content structure, card sorting
- Interaction design: flows, states, micro-interactions, prototyping
- Visual design: typography, color theory, layout, hierarchy, branding
- Tooling: Figma (primary), prototyping, design hand-off
- Usability heuristics and design principles
- Accessibility (a11y): WCAG, inclusive design, contrast, keyboard/screen-reader support
- Design systems: components, tokens, patterns, documentation
- Collaboration with product and engineering; design critique

## Sub-roles / Specializations
- UX Designer (research- and flow-focused)
- UI / Visual Designer (aesthetics and brand)
- Interaction / Motion Designer
- Product Designer (end-to-end)
- Design Systems Designer
- UX Writer / Content Designer (adjacent)
- Lead / Staff / Principal Designer and Design Manager (leadership track)

## Salary Trend
US (USD): entry ~$75,000; mid ~$105,000–$130,000; senior/staff ~$150,000–$210,000+ (higher at large tech firms with equity). India note: compensation typically ranges ₹5–22 LPA depending on experience and company tier. 2025–26 demand: steady demand with growing emphasis on design systems, accessibility, and designers who can work fluently alongside AI tooling; generalist product designers and design-system specialists are especially sought after.

## Learning Roadmap
**Beginner:** Learn design fundamentals — typography, color, layout, hierarchy. Master Figma basics. Study core usability heuristics and complete the Google UX Design Certificate. Redesign a simple app screen and gather informal feedback.
**Intermediate:** Run usability tests, build wireframes-to-high-fidelity prototypes, learn information architecture and interaction patterns. Apply WCAG accessibility. Build a portfolio with 2–3 case studies showing process, not just final visuals.
**Advanced:** Design and maintain a scalable design system, lead end-to-end product design, facilitate critiques and workshops, and influence product strategy. Develop a specialization (motion, systems, research-heavy).

## Notes for Course Generation
Structure the course as modules: design foundations (visual + heuristics), Figma and prototyping, UX research and usability, information architecture and interaction design, accessibility, and design systems. Prerequisites are minimal — visual sensibility and curiosity about users; no coding required, though HTML/CSS awareness helps. Include hands-on projects: a heuristic evaluation, an end-to-end app redesign case study with usability test results, and a small reusable component library, culminating in a portfolio-ready capstone.
