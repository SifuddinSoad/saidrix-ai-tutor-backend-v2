# Product Manager

## Overview
A Product Manager (PM) owns the "why" and "what" of a product: identifying customer problems worth solving, defining the product vision and strategy, and orchestrating cross-functional teams (engineering, design, data, marketing, sales) to ship outcomes that drive business value. PMs operate at the intersection of user needs, technical feasibility, and business viability. They are responsible for product discovery, prioritization, roadmap definition, and continuously measuring impact through metrics. The role is less about authority and more about influence — aligning stakeholders around a shared direction without direct reporting lines over most contributors.

## Required Skills
- Product discovery: customer interviews, problem validation, opportunity assessment
- Prioritization frameworks: RICE, value vs. effort, opportunity scoring, Kano
- Roadmapping: outcome-based roadmaps, sequencing, stakeholder communication
- Metrics and analytics: defining KPIs/North Star metrics, funnel analysis, A/B testing, SQL basics
- Communication and storytelling: written PRDs, executive updates, alignment narratives
- Strategy: market analysis, competitive positioning, business model understanding
- Collaboration: working with engineering and design, agile/scrum rituals
- Customer empathy and user research literacy

## Sub-roles / Specializations
- Associate Product Manager (APM) — entry-level rotational role
- Growth PM — acquisition, activation, retention, monetization loops
- Platform / API PM — internal platforms, developer-facing products
- Technical PM — deeply technical products (infra, ML, data)
- Group Product Manager / Director of Product / VP Product — leadership track
- Product-led Growth (PLG) PM, AI/ML PM, B2B vs. B2C PM

## Salary Trend
US (USD, base + total comp varies): entry/APM ~$100,000; mid ~$140,000–$170,000; senior/staff ~$190,000–$300,000+ (FAANG and senior leadership can exceed this with equity). India note: senior PM compensation typically ranges ₹12–40 LPA depending on company tier and experience. 2025–26 demand: strong but more competitive at entry level; AI product management, growth, and platform PM roles see the highest demand and premium compensation.

## Learning Roadmap
**Beginner:** Learn product fundamentals — what PMs do, the product lifecycle, agile basics. Practice writing simple PRDs and user stories. Study a product you use daily and critique it. Take an intro certificate (Google Project Management).
**Intermediate:** Run real customer discovery interviews, build prioritization models, define metrics and instrument analytics. Learn A/B testing and basic SQL. Ship a side project or contribute to a real roadmap. Study "Inspired" by Marty Cagan.
**Advanced:** Own product strategy and vision, manage stakeholder politics, drive multi-quarter roadmaps, mentor junior PMs. Deepen domain expertise (AI, growth, platform) and develop business/financial acumen.

## Notes for Course Generation
A course should be modular: foundations (role, lifecycle, agile), discovery (interviews, problem framing), prioritization and roadmapping, metrics and experimentation, and strategy/leadership. Prerequisites are minimal — basic analytical thinking and familiarity with software products help; SQL and analytics can be taught inline. Include hands-on projects such as writing a full PRD, conducting and synthesizing user interviews, and building a prioritized roadmap with a North Star metric, plus a capstone case study analyzing a real product's growth or pivot.
