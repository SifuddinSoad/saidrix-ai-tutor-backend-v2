# Cybersecurity Analyst (SOC Analyst)

## Overview
A Cybersecurity Analyst (commonly a Security Operations Center / SOC Analyst) is the front line of an organization's defense. The role centers on continuously monitoring networks, endpoints, and cloud environments for malicious activity, triaging and investigating alerts, responding to incidents, and reducing the attack surface through vulnerability management. Analysts work with SIEM platforms, EDR/XDR tooling, and threat intelligence feeds to detect, contain, and remediate threats, then document findings and improve detection rules over time. The position is foundational in the cybersecurity career path and is one of the most common entry points into the field, with strong upward mobility into incident response, threat hunting, detection engineering, and security engineering. Most analysts progress through Tier 1 (triage), Tier 2 (investigation), and Tier 3 (threat hunting/forensics).

## Required Skills
- Networking fundamentals (TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP/S, routing, firewalls, VPNs)
- Operating system internals (Windows event logs, Active Directory, Linux syslog)
- SIEM operation and log analysis (Splunk, Microsoft Sentinel, Elastic, QRadar)
- Endpoint detection and response (EDR/XDR) and host-based investigation
- Incident response process (NIST SP 800-61 lifecycle: prepare, detect, contain, eradicate, recover)
- Vulnerability management (scanning, prioritization, remediation tracking)
- Threat intelligence and adversary behavior analysis (MITRE ATT&CK mapping)
- Scripting/automation basics (Python, PowerShell, KQL/SPL query languages)
- Foundational packet analysis (Wireshark) and basic malware/phishing analysis
- Clear written and verbal communication for incident reporting and escalation

## Sub-roles / Specializations
- SOC Analyst (Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3)
- Incident Responder / Digital Forensics & Incident Response (DFIR)
- Threat Hunter
- Detection Engineer (writing and tuning detection rules)
- Threat Intelligence Analyst (CTI)
- Vulnerability Management Analyst
- SIEM/SOAR Engineer

## Salary Trend
- US entry-level: ~$80,000 USD
- US mid-level: ~$110,000–$135,000 USD
- US senior-level: ~$150,000–$200,000+ USD
- India: roughly ₹5–22 LPA depending on experience, location, and certifications
- 2025–26 demand: Persistently high. SOC analyst remains one of the most in-demand cybersecurity roles globally, with a continued workforce shortage and steady hiring through 2025–2026 as organizations expand detection and response capacity. AI-assisted triage is reshaping but not reducing demand.

## Learning Roadmap
**Beginner:** Build networking and OS fundamentals; learn the CIA triad and common attack types (phishing, malware, brute force). Earn **CompTIA Security+ (SY0-701)**. Practice hands-on with guided labs (TryHackMe SOC Level 1 path, Hack The Box Academy beginner modules). Learn to read Windows/Linux logs and basic Wireshark captures.

**Intermediate:** Master a SIEM (Splunk or Microsoft Sentinel) and query languages (SPL/KQL). Study the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and the incident response lifecycle. Map detections to MITRE ATT&CK. Earn **CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-003)** — note CS0-004 launches in early 2026 with CS0-003 retiring around June 2026. Optionally pursue Blue Team Level 1 (BTL1) or Microsoft SC-200.

**Advanced:** Specialize in DFIR, threat hunting, or detection engineering. Learn memory/disk forensics, threat-intel pipelines, and SOAR automation. Pursue advanced certifications such as GIAC GCIH/GCFA, and progress toward leadership-track credentials like CISSP.

## Notes for Course Generation
Structure the course as progressive modules following the SOC tier progression: networking and OS fundamentals, log analysis, SIEM operation, the incident response lifecycle, vulnerability management, and threat intelligence with MITRE ATT&CK mapping. Prerequisites are basic IT literacy and networking; no prior security experience required for the beginner track. Include hands-on lab projects such as building and querying a SIEM dashboard, triaging a simulated phishing/malware alert end-to-end, and writing an incident report with ATT&CK technique attribution.
